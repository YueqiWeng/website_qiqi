Teaching & Mentorship

My passion for teaching has developed through experiences across educational technology, learning design, science education, and language learning. Over the course of my graduate studies, I have had opportunities to teach and support learners in a variety of settings, from adult language education and undergraduate science courses to graduate-level learning design courses. At the University of Georgia, I currently serve as the instructor of record for Teaching and Learning With Technology, where I independently design and teach an undergraduate course focused on the meaningful integration of technology into teaching and learning. I have also supported graduate students in Design Interactive Learning Environments and previously co-taught undergraduate biology courses at Purdue University.

These experiences have shaped my identity as both an educator and a learning experience designer. Because my research focuses on human-centered technology, artificial intelligence, and digital learning environments, I strive to connect my research with my teaching by helping students critically examine not only how technologies work, but how and why they should be used to support learning. My goal is to help students become thoughtful users, designers, and evaluators of technology who can make informed decisions about its role in education. I also aim to create accessible learning environments in which students can experiment, receive meaningful feedback, revise their work, and connect course concepts with problems that matter beyond the classroom.

Teaching Philosophy

I believe effective teaching begins with creating a supportive, inclusive, and student-centered learning environment where students feel comfortable exploring ideas, asking questions, and learning through practice. My teaching emphasizes authentic tasks, active learning, reflection, and constructive feedback. Rather than teaching technology as a set of isolated tools, I encourage students to consider how technology can be meaningfully and responsibly used to address real learning needs. Ultimately, I hope to help students develop both the confidence to use technology and the critical thinking needed to make thoughtful design and instructional decisions.
